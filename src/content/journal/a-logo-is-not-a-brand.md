Every few months someone asks us for "a new logo". Almost every time, what they actually want is to be understood.

That's not a criticism. A logo is a tangible thing. You can hold it up in a meeting, you can approve it, you can put it on a van. Positioning, tone of voice and the decision about who you are *not* for are much harder to point at — so they get skipped, and the logo is asked to do work it was never designed to do.

## The logo is a signature, not a speech

A good mark does one job: it signs the work. It tells people that this thing came from you. What it cannot do is explain why you matter, what you believe or why anyone should choose you over the business next door. That's the job of everything around it — the words, the pictures, the way you answer the phone.

When a brand feels weak, the logo is the most visible suspect and the least likely culprit.

## What we design first

Before we draw anything, we try to write one honest sentence about the company that a competitor could not say about themselves. If we can't, no amount of typography will save it. If we can, the visual identity tends to arrive quickly, because it finally has something to express.

Then we design the system — type, colour, image style, layout rules, the way the brand moves and speaks. The logo comes near the end, when we know what it needs to sign.

## A useful test

Cover the logo on your website, your last campaign and your social feed. Could someone still tell it was you?

If the answer is yes, you have a brand. If the answer is no, a new logo won't change it. But the right brief will.
