It's worth starting from an uncomfortable truth: almost everyone, almost all of the time, would rather not be looking at your advertising. They were doing something else. You interrupted them.

That isn't a reason to stop advertising. It's a reason to make the interruption worth it.

## Be interesting before you are informative

People give you a fraction of a second to decide whether to keep looking. In that time, the feature list doesn't matter. What matters is whether the work makes them curious, makes them smile or makes them feel seen. Information can come second — and it lands far better when attention has been earned first.

## One idea, said well

The temptation in every brief is to say everything: the price, the quality, the heritage, the offer, the sustainability credentials. The result is an ad that says nothing memorable. Pick the one thing that matters most to the person seeing it, and say it in a way only you could.

## Respect the context

A line that works on a poster across the street can feel loud on a phone at midnight. The same idea needs different manners in different places. We plan placements and creative together for exactly that reason: the medium changes how the message is heard.

## Consistency is a creative choice

Brands are remembered through repetition. Not the same ad over and over, but the same voice, the same visual world, the same attitude. A campaign that looks different every month asks the audience to start again every month.

Make the interruption worth it. Then make it recognisable.
