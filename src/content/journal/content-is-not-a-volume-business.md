At some point, a lot of brands decided that the solution to social media was *more*. More posts, more formats, more trends to jump on. The result is often a feed that is busy, inconsistent and easy to ignore.

## Think in series, not posts

A single post has to fight for attention on its own. A series builds an expectation. When people recognise a format — a recurring question, a type of story, a visual style — they start to look for the next one. That's the difference between content people scroll past and content people follow.

## Fewer, better formats

Pick a small number of formats you can make well and sustainably. Design them properly, with a clear structure and a recognisable look. Then let the ideas inside them change. Constraints make content faster to produce and easier to recognise.

## Make it for the sound-off scroll

Most people meet your content silently, on a small screen, while doing something else. The first frame has to work on its own. Captions should read like a person wrote them. Text on screen should be big enough to read without squinting.

## Measure what matters

Reach and likes are easy to count, but they aren't always the point. Saves, shares, replies and the questions people ask tell you far more about whether the content is doing its job. Look at those first.

The goal isn't to be everywhere. It's to be worth finding.
